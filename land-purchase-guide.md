# Houston-Region Land Purchase Decision Guide
**Path A:** small plot, RV-only (fallback, may be bought first and held) · **Path B:** 2–5 acres, multiple houses + RV, family co-owned (primary, 12–24 mo.)

> Not legal advice. Written from general knowledge of Texas law, not live research. Statutes, fees, and county programs change, so confirm every citation and number with the named office before relying on it. Have a Texas real-estate attorney review before you sign anything for Path B.

## 1. Identify the jurisdiction stack first (free, ~1 hour per parcel)
Almost every answer below depends on this. Don't assume "Harris County rules" until you've confirmed which layers apply.

| Layer | Why it matters | How to find it | Office |
|---|---|---|---|
| County | Texas counties generally have **no zoning power** [K]. They do control platting, floodplain, driveways/culverts, and septic (directly or via an agent) [J]. | Appraisal district parcel search | Harris: Harris County Engineering Dept. (Permit Office); other counties: county engineer / road & bridge / health dept. |
| City limits / ETJ | Inside a city, its zoning (if any) and building code apply. In the ETJ (extraterritorial jurisdiction, up to ~5 miles for Houston [K]) the city typically controls **platting/subdivision**, not zoning [K]. | City GIS or planning dept. map | City planning dept.; for Houston, Planning & Development Dept. |
| Special districts (MUD, WCID, PUD, LID) | Extra property tax, possible mandatory service, and a required purchaser notice at closing (Water Code §49.452 [K]). | Taxing-unit list on the appraisal-district account | HCAD (Harris), Fort Bend CAD, Montgomery CAD, etc.; TCEQ district records |
| Flood | FEMA zone, county floodplain permit, post-Harvey elevation rules stricter than FEMA minimums in Harris [J] | msc.fema.gov; HCFCD flood mapping tool | County floodplain administrator; Harris County Flood Control District |
| Groundwater / subsidence | Well permits/limits [J] | Parcel location | Harris-Galveston Subsidence District; Fort Bend Subsidence District; Lone Star GCD (Montgomery); Brazoria County GCD |
| Septic authority (OSSF) | Who permits septic [J] | TCEQ OSSF program page lists the authorized agent per county | County dept. or TCEQ regional office |
| Electric | Utility varies: CenterPoint, Entergy Texas, AEP Texas, or a co-op [J] | Ask neighbors; PUC map | The wires utility (TDU), not the retail provider |
| Roads | County-maintained, TxDOT, private, or city street decides who permits the driveway and who plows/repairs [V] | County road-maintenance list; TxDOT | Harris Co. Engineering; TxDOT district office |
| Deed restrictions / HOA | **The real land-use control in unzoned Texas** [K] | Title commitment + County Clerk | County Clerk real-property records |

## 2. Restriction checklist, side by side
"Disqualifier" = the answer that should kill the parcel for that path unless it is cured in writing before closing.

### 2a. Title, legal, ownership
| # | Item | Why it matters | Proof / office | Path A disqualifier | Path B disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Recorded deed restrictions / covenants** [K] | Private covenants can ban RVs, "temporary" or mobile dwellings, secondary dwellings, subdivision, small houses. Neighbors/HOA can sue for an injunction + fees. | Title commitment Schedule B (get a copy of *every* instrument listed); County Clerk records; plat notes | Bars RV living or storage/parking > X days, trailers, "temporary structures" | Single-family-only, one dwelling per lot, no manufactured/tiny homes, min. lot size > your tract, bars further division |
| 2 | HOA / POA, ACC approval, dues [K: Prop. Code ch. 209] | Approval power over structures; assessment liens; fines | HOA docs, resale certificate, management company | RV prohibited or not visible from the street | ACC approval for multiple dwellings unobtainable or discretionary |
| 3 | Boundaries / survey | Encroachments, gaps, and acreage shortfalls surface here | Current survey (not the seller's old one); title survey exception | Unresolvable encroachment on the pad area | Same, plus usable (non-easement, non-floodway) acreage < what the plan needs |
| 4 | **Legal access** [V] | Landlocked or handshake access = unusable and unfinanceable | Plat; recorded easement; title policy ingress/egress coverage; county road list | No recorded legal access | Same, plus access easement language limits it to "one residence," is too narrow for fire apparatus, or has no road-maintenance agreement |
| 5 | **Platting / legal-lot status** [K: Loc. Gov't Code ch. 232; J] | An unplatted carve-out can be refused utilities, permits, and title insurance. Dividing land later for family members may trigger platting. | Harris Co. Engineering (platting/permits) or the county equivalent; County Clerk plat records; city if in ETJ | Illegal subdivision with no cure path (utilities/permits refused) | Same, plus the plan needs separate lots per family and platting is infeasible (frontage, drainage, road standards) |
| 6 | Mineral & surface rights | Severed minerals are common; operators may have surface-use rights | Title commitment exceptions; Railroad Commission online map (wells, pipelines) | Active/planned drill site or pipeline across pad | Same, plus pipeline/oil easements through buildable area |
| 7 | Easements & ROW (utility, pipeline, drainage, flood-control) | Can eat the buildable area; setbacks apply | Survey; title Schedule B; HCFCD | Easement through the only usable pad | Easements leave < required buildable area for all houses + septic fields + well |
| 8 | Taxes, special assessments, ag-valuation rollback [K: Tax Code §23.55] | Delinquent taxes follow the land; changing from ag use triggers rollback taxes (3 yrs) | County tax office; appraisal district; MUD/PID notice | Unpayable delinquency; PID/MUD debt out of scale for a small plot | Same; plus ag rollback you can't absorb when you add houses |
| 9 | Seller authority / chain of title | Heirs' property, unpaid co-owners, spousal homestead claims void deals | Title commitment; probate records | Any owner/heir not signing | Same |
| 10 | Contract type [K: Prop. Code ch. 5, subch. D] | Contract-for-deed gives weak buyer protection | Contract; title company | Contract-for-deed with no recorded protection | Same |
| 11 | **Co-ownership structure** | Any tenant-in-common can force partition [K]; one family member's divorce/death/creditor can freeze the deal | Attorney-drafted co-ownership/LLC agreement; title company vesting | n/a | Any member refuses a **signed agreement before closing**; no buy-out/exit rules |
| 12 | Financing per house / homestead [K: Tex. Const. art. XVI §50] | Individual mortgages generally need a separate legal lot; homestead rules constrain liens | Lender; attorney | n/a | Plan requires separate mortgages but subdividing is impossible |

### 2b. Land use, building, occupancy
| # | Item | Why it matters | Proof / office | Path A disqualifier | Path B disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | City zoning (only if in city limits) | Unzoned ≠ unregulated, but inside a zoned city the zoning rules | City planning dept. zoning verification letter | Zoning bars RV occupancy or RV storage | Zoning bars multiple dwellings / sets density below plan |
| 14 | County/city rules on **RV occupancy** [J] | Some jurisdictions cap days of RV living, require an RV-park license, or require connection to septic | County Engineering/permits; city code enforcement; county health dept. | Written prohibition on full- or part-time living, whichever you plan | RV may be counted as a dwelling toward the dwelling cap |
| 15 | **Multiple-dwelling rules** [J] | >1 dwelling may be classed as a subdivision, manufactured-home community, multifamily, or RV park, triggering platting, fire, septic, and utility rules | County Engineering; fire marshal; OSSF agent; utility | n/a | Classification you can't meet in budget/timeline |
| 16 | Dwelling type | Site-built vs. HUD manufactured vs. park-model RV vs. tiny house on wheels are regulated differently (manufactured homes: TDHCA titling/installation [K]) | Deed restrictions; county; TDHCA Manufactured Housing; lender | Restrictions bar the RV class you own (e.g., age/condition limits) | Restrictions bar your chosen dwelling types |
| 17 | Building permit/inspection regime [J] | Unincorporated counties have limited building-code power [K]; utilities may still demand inspection/permit before energizing | County; utility; city if in ETJ/limits | Utility will not energize without a permit you can't get | Same, per house |
| 18 | Fire code / emergency access [J] | Multiple dwellings may trigger fire-marshal review, turnaround, hydrant/fire-flow | Harris Co. Fire Marshal / local ESD or fire dept. | n/a | Road/turnaround/fire-flow requirements you can't meet |
| 19 | Driveway / culvert permit | County or TxDOT permit; sight distance; culvert sizing | Harris Co. Engineering permit office; TxDOT district | Permit denied / no workable approach | Multiple driveways or shared drive not approvable |

### 2c. Flood, environment
| # | Item | Why it matters | Proof / office | Path A disqualifier | Path B disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | **Flood zone & history** | Floodplain development permit and elevation rules; insurance cost; repeated flooding | FEMA FIRM; HCFCD tools; county floodplain administrator; ask seller/neighbors for flood history and insurance claims | Floodway, or pad and access routinely underwater | Buildable area mostly floodplain and required elevation/fill/no-rise rules make houses impractical or cost-prohibitive |
| 21 | Drainage / fill limits [J] | Fill may be restricted; you can't push water onto neighbors | County floodplain admin; HCFCD | Pad can't be raised/drained legally | Same, ×N houses |
| 22 | Wetlands / environmental | Federal wetlands (USACE §404), prior contamination, pits, landfill, oilfield waste | National Wetlands Inventory; Phase I ESA; RRC records | Contamination or wetlands on the pad | Same on any building/septic/well area |

### 2d. Utilities (the usual deal-killers)
| # | Item | Why it matters | Proof / office | Path A disqualifier | Path B disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23 | **Electric** | Extension cost can exceed the land's value; each house needs its own meter or a submetering plan | Written service-availability + cost quote from the TDU; permit/inspection requirements | Not served or extension cost beyond your cap; can't get RV-appropriate service (30/50A) | Same; or multiple meters not allowed on one tract; transformer/line capacity for N houses |
| 24 | **Water** | Public tap vs. private well; groundwater rules | Provider will-serve letter; groundwater/subsidence district permit status; neighbor well depth/quality | No water source feasible | Same at N-house demand. | 25 | **Wastewater** | RV holding-tank hauling vs. OSSF vs. sewer. | 26 | Internet / cell / trash | Livability, not legality | ISP availability map; county | (soft) | (soft) |
| 27 | **Insurability** | Wind (TWIA for coastal counties [J]), flood, RV, liability | Written quotes before closing | Uninsurable or unaffordable | Same, per house |

## 3. Items that differ between paths
| Situation | Path A (RV) | Path B (houses + RV) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small lot in a restricted subdivision allowing RVs | Often fine | **Blocked**: one dwelling per lot, min. lot size | Covenants |
| Covenant bans "temporary structures"/trailers but allows houses | **Blocked** | Fine | Covenants |
| Lot < ~½ acre | Fine if holding tank or sewer connection | **Blocked**: OSSF area, well/septic setbacks, no room for multiple systems | Septic/well geometry |
| Utility extension cost $15–40k+ | Often not worth it for one RV | Can be justified when spread over several houses | Fixed infrastructure cost |
| Access easement says "one residence" | Fine | **Blocked** | Easement scope |
| Floodplain parcel | RV (movable, licensed, highway-ready) may be tolerated [J] | Fixed houses need elevation/fill/permits | Floodplain permit rules |
| Single meter, one drive, one well | Fine | **Blocked** / needs redesign | Per-dwelling service |
| Parcel is a carve-out from a larger tract | Sometimes tolerated | Subdivision/platting issues when family splits | Ch. 232 [J] |
| Inside city limits | Possible nuisance/zoning problem for RV living | Possible zoning density limits | Zoning |
| Remote, no utilities, 3 acres | Cheap if self-contained (solar/holding tank/hauled water) | Needs real infrastructure + shared-utility agreements | Scale |
| Raw-land financing | Smaller loan, often cash | Lender may refuse multi-owner, unplatted, or shared-infrastructure land | Underwriting |
| Resale | Easy only if legally RV-capable | Narrower buyer pool | Marketability |
| Ag valuation (1-d-1) | Usually irrelevant | Houses end or shrink it; rollback taxes apply | Tax Code |

## 4. Go / no-go gate sequence
Each gate has a **stop condition**. Do not pay for the next gate until the previous one passes. Spending rises as you go.

| Gate | Action | Cost | Stop if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Get the parcel ID; identify county, city/ETJ, MUD/special districts, flood zone, road type (Section 1) | Free | In a zoned city that bars your use; floodway; no road frontage |
| **1** | Pull *all* recorded deed restrictions and the plat from the County Clerk. Read them yourself. | Free–small copy fees | Any item 1/2 disqualifier |
| **2** | Call county engineering/permits: legal-lot/platting status, driveway permit, RV occupancy rules, multiple-dwelling rules, OSSF authority (items 5, 14, 15, 19, 25) | Free | Illegal subdivision with no cure; occupancy or multi-dwelling rule fails |
| **3** | Flood: FEMA map, HCFCD tools, county floodplain administrator conversation, seller flood/claim history (items 20–21) | Free | Floodway or chronic flooding on the pad / buildable area |
| **4** | Utility letters: electric will-serve + cost; water provider or groundwater-district well status; septic agent's pre-application answers (items 23–25) | Free–nominal | Costs exceed your cap; no feasible water or wastewater path |
| **5** | **Sign a contract with a negotiated option period, ample title-objection time, and survey/inspection contingencies**; order the title commitment; read Schedule B | Option fee + earnest money (at risk) | Any title/covenant/access disqualifier in the commitment |
| **6** | Licensed site evaluator / soil evaluation for septic; get the authorized agent's written feedback (item 25) | Low hundreds to ~$1,500 (est.) | Soils fail or acreage can't fit required systems |
| **7** | Survey (boundary + topo/flood elevation), ordered with title company input | ~$1.5k–$5k+ (est.) | Encroachments, shortfalls, easements eating the buildable area |
| **8** | Phase I ESA *only if* a red flag (oilfield, dumping, adjacent industrial) | ~$2.5k–$5k (est.) | Contamination |
| **9 (B only)** | Family: signed co-ownership/LLC agreement, funding schedule, exit/buy-out terms, attorney review; lender position confirmed | Attorney fees | Anyone refuses or terms don't close |
| **10** | **Only now**: engineering, site plan, design, well/septic design | Largest spend | n/a |

**Path A shortcut:** Gates 0–8 apply. 6–7 can be lighter if the plot is already platted with sewer/water.
**Path B:** Gate 9 is non-negotiable before closing.

## 4a. How to run Gate 0 on a listing (~20 min, free)
Capture first: address or parcel ID, acreage, county, road, and the listing's claims about utilities, restrictions, and flood. A listing site's property ID is **not** a county account number. Get the county account number in step 1.

| Step | Where | Record | Stop if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Parcel lookup | County appraisal district (HCAD Harris; Montgomery CAD for Montgomery; search "[county] appraisal district"). Search by address, owner, or map click. | Account number, legal description (lot/block or abstract), acreage, owner name, **taxing units** (MUD, PID, city, ESD), land-use code, ag valuation, delinquent taxes | Owner ≠ seller; acreage differs materially from the listing; delinquent taxes; unexpected MUD/PID |
| 2. City / ETJ | City planning dept. map ("[city] ETJ map"), or the city on the taxing-unit list | In city limits, in ETJ, or neither | In city limits: get a zoning verification before going further |
| 3. Flood | msc.fema.gov (enter address); county/Harris flood mapping tools; Google Earth Pro historical imagery set to Sept 2017 (Harvey) and Sept 2019 (Imelda) | FEMA zone (X, AE, A, floodway); imagery of standing water; whether the pad is high ground | Floodway, or visible chronic flooding on pad or access |
| 4. Access | Street View and Google Earth; appraisal-district map for frontage; county road-maintenance list | Frontage on a public road, or an easement route; road maintained by county/private | No frontage and no recorded easement visible |
| 5. Restrictions | County Clerk real-property index (search the subdivision name, or the parent tract's owner for metes-and-bounds parcels) | Any "Restrictions," "Declaration," "Covenants" hits; HOA existence | Restrictions found that ban your use (read them in Gate 1). "Unrestricted" in a listing is marketing, not proof. |
| 6. Wells / pipelines | Railroad Commission public GIS viewer | Wells or pipelines on or adjacent to the parcel | Pipeline or well pad on the only usable area |
| 7. Utility hints | Street View (poles, meters, hydrants); MUD name from step 1 | Likely electric utility, likely water provider | Nothing nearby for a long way. Not an automatic kill; flag for Gate 4. |

**Scoring:** mark each step Pass / Fail / Unknown. Any Fail ends the listing. Unknowns carry into Gates 1–4. Spend nothing until Gate 0 has no Fails.

## 5. Questions to ask (literal wording)

### County (Harris: Engineering Dept. Permit Office; other counties: county engineer / road & bridge / health or environmental dept.)
1. "For parcel [ID], is this a legally platted lot, or a metes-and-bounds tract? Can you confirm that in writing or point me to the recorded plat?"
2. "If one owner holds [N] acres and wants to put [N] houses on it for family members without selling lots, does the county treat that as a subdivision requiring a plat?"
3. "Does the county limit RVs used as living quarters on private land? Any maximum days, permit, or requirement to be connected to a septic system?"
4. "Is there a cap on how many dwellings, or any RVs, can be on one tract without triggering manufactured-home-community, RV-park, or multifamily rules?"
5. "Who is the authorized agent for on-site sewage in this area, and what is the minimum lot area per dwelling for a permitted system on [public water / private well]?"
6. "Is the parcel in a floodplain or floodway? What elevation is required for a house and for an RV pad? Is fill restricted?"
7. "Is the road county-maintained, TxDOT, or private? What driveway/culvert permit applies, and are there access-spacing limits?"
8. "Is this parcel in a city's ETJ or city limits? Which city, and does that city require its own plat approval?"
9. "Will the fire marshal or ESD require anything for more than one dwelling (access width, turnaround, hydrant, fire flow)?"
10. "Are there any open code violations, liens, or permits on this parcel?"

### Seller
1. "Do you hold the minerals? Have they been reserved or leased to anyone?"
2. "Has the property ever flooded? Have there been insurance or federal flood claims?"
3. "Do you have a recent survey and the title policy from when you bought it?"
4. "How do you access the property? Is the access recorded, and who maintains the road?"
5. "Are there any deed restrictions or HOA? May I see the documents now?"
6. "Has a septic system, well, or electric service ever been permitted or installed? Do you have the records?"
7. "Has anyone ever lived on or placed an RV or structure here, and was it ever cited?"
8. "Are all owners/heirs on the deed willing to sign? Are any taxes, liens, or assessments outstanding?"
9. "Will you agree to an option period of [X] days and to closing contingent on utility and septic confirmation?"
10. "Are you offering owner financing? If so, is it a recorded deed of trust or a contract-for-deed?"

### Title company
1. "Please send the commitment and copies of **every** instrument listed in Schedule B before I go under contract, or immediately after."
2. "Does the commitment include insured access (ingress/egress), and can you remove the area-and-boundary exception with a survey?"
3. "Are there any restrictive covenants, easements, or mineral reservations that limit residential or RV use?"
4. "How should title be vested for [family members/LLC], and what does each option do to future financing?"
5. "Are there any MUD/PID/special-district notices required at closing?"
6. "Is the tract a legal subdivision for title-insurance purposes? Any exceptions on that point?"
7. "What do you need to see for heirs, spouses, or an estate if the seller isn't the sole record owner?"
8. "What endorsements are available for this land, and what do they cost?"

### Family co-owners
1. "How much is each person contributing now, and how much is each committed to over the next 24 months?"
2. "What happens if someone can't pay, wants out, divorces, or dies? Who has buy-out rights, and at what price formula?"
3. "Who decides on utilities, shared infrastructure, new structures, and dispute resolution?"
4. "Will we hold as tenants in common or through an LLC? Will each family need its own mortgage, which would require a separate legal lot?"
5. "Which house gets which part of the land, and is that written down, not just agreed to?"
6. "If Path B takes 24 months, are we all okay buying Path A first and selling or repurposing it afterward?"
7. "Would anyone object to a signed agreement reviewed by a Texas real-estate attorney before we close?"

### Utilities / districts (extras)
1. Electric: "What is the cost and timeline to energize parcel [ID]? Is a permit/inspection needed? Can I have separate meters for [N] dwellings?"
2. Water/MUD: "Does this tract have water and sewer availability? What is the tap fee and tax rate? Is a will-serve letter available?"
3. Groundwater/subsidence district: "Is a permit or exemption required for a residential well here? Any restrictions on new wells?"

## 7. Red flags that end the deal now
- No recorded legal access, or access depends on a verbal easement.
- Deed restrictions prohibit your intended use (RV living / multiple dwellings), and the seller or HOA won't amend in writing.
- Floodway parcel, or repeated flooding on the pad/access route.
- County or utility says "we will not serve/permit this" and the seller won't cure it before closing.
- Illegal subdivision with no plat path.
- Seller refuses an option period, survey, title commitment, or inspection access.
- Contract-for-deed or unrecorded seller financing without attorney review.
- Seller isn't the sole record owner, or heirs aren't all signing.
- Pressure to close fast or to waive title/survey contingencies.
- Active oil/gas site, pipeline through buildable area, or unclear environmental history.
- Soil evaluation says septic can't be permitted for your use.
- Insurer won't quote, or flood/wind insurance makes the plan unaffordable.
- Path B: any family member won't sign a co-ownership agreement before closing.
- Utility extension or septic cost exceeds your pre-set budget cap.

## 8. Buy-A-first, hold-for-B tips
- Buy A only if it passes Gates 0–8 on its own merits. Don't assume it's "just a holding parcel." A parcel that's legally RV-capable is also easier to resell.
- Write down the maximum hold period, carrying cost, and the exit plan (sell, keep as guest/RV site, or fold into B).
- Keep A's title clean and separate from the family entity for B.
- Check whether A's tax status, insurance, and any deed restrictions allow long-term unattended storage of an RV.
- If B ends up adjacent, don't count A's acreage toward B's septic/setback math without a survey and agent confirmation.

## 9. First five verification steps (do these in order)
1. **Identify the stack for each parcel:** county, city/ETJ, MUD/special district, flood zone, road ownership (Section 1). Free; kills many parcels immediately.
2. **Pull and read the recorded deed restrictions and plat from the County Clerk, plus HOA docs.** The primary RV/multi-dwelling control.
3. **Call county engineering/permits** with the Section 5 county questions: legal-lot status, RV occupancy, multiple-dwelling, OSSF authority, driveway.
4. **Get written utility confirmations:** electric will-serve and cost, water source (provider or well-permit status), and septic-agent guidance.
5. **Go under contract with an option period and get the title commitment;** read every Schedule B instrument before ordering the survey, soil evaluation, or any design.

## 10. Financing by land use
Rates, limits, and eligibility change. Treat this as structure and get written quotes. Lenders finance by **use**, and land-only loans are the most expensive money.

| Use | Typical products | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Raw land (nothing built) | Community banks, credit unions, Farm Credit lenders, seller financing | Often 20–50% down, shorter terms (5–20 yr), balloons, higher rates than a mortgage |
| Land + one home (buy and build) | Construction-to-permanent loans (FHA, VA, conventional, USDA, Farm Credit "country home") | Cheapest long-term money, but built around **one primary dwelling** |
| Land with existing house | Standard mortgage; USDA / FHA / VA / conventional | Easiest. Consider buying a tract with a house and adding the others later. |
| Multiple houses, one parcel | Per-house mortgages only if each house is on a **separate legal lot**; otherwise blanket or commercial/LLC loan, or family-funded equity | The hard part of Path B |
| RV only (Path A) | Land loan or cash for the lot; separate RV loan for the vehicle | RV loans don't finance land. A lot with an RV is raw land to a lender. |
| Real farming operation | USDA FSA farm-ownership loans, Farm Credit | Only for a genuine ag business |

### USDA
- **Section 502 Guaranteed:** zero down, for a home in a USDA-eligible rural area, with household income limits. Finances **land + home**, not land alone; generally expects a single-family primary residence. Check property eligibility by address on USDA's site; many Houston suburbs are ineligible, some outlying areas qualify. Ask the lender whether other structures on the parcel are allowed.
- **Section 502 Direct:** subsidized, for low and very-low income households; limited funds. Ask your local Rural Development office.
- **FSA farm loans:** for actual farm operations (including beginning-farmer and down-payment programs); require a farm business plan.
- **Rule of thumb:** USDA fits a single-home build on a clean legal lot. It does not fit multiple houses on one tract.

### Other sources
- **Texas Veterans Land Board land loan** (if a buyer is a Texas veteran): rural land, low down payment. Verify current terms, minimum acreage, and city-limit restrictions.
- **VA / FHA:** home-focused; finance land only as part of a home purchase or construction via approved lenders.
- **Farm Credit lenders** (e.g., Capital Farm Credit, Lone Star Ag Credit; confirm which serves your county): usually the best fit for 2–5 acre rural land and country homes.
- **Texas home-equity cash-out / HELOC on an existing home:** often the cheapest down-payment source, but Texas caps home-equity debt at 80% of value [K] and the homestead rules are strict. Confirm with a lender/attorney.
- **Seller financing:** only via a recorded deed of trust. Avoid contract-for-deed.
- **Manufactured homes:** often chattel loans unless converted to real property under Texas titling rules. Attorney input needed.
- **Cash / family funds:** often the only practical route for multi-house, multi-owner structures. Document gifts with letters and loans with written agreements.

### Getting the best terms
1. Buy a legal, platted, access-clean, flood-clean parcel. Lenders reject exactly what Gates 0–2 screen out.
2. Match the loan to the use. Don't put a home build on a raw-land loan.
3. Get 3–4 quotes in the same week: your bank/credit union, a Farm Credit lender, a mortgage broker (USDA, FHA, VA), and a community bank. Compare APR, down payment, term, prepayment penalty, and balloon date.
4. Pre-qualify before offering. Ask each lender what they require on appraisal, survey, and acreage.
5. For Path B ask up front: "Can you lend against one house on a separate lot? What do you need if title is held by an LLC or multiple relatives?"
6. Buy Path A in cash or low-leverage debt if you may sell. A balloon due in 12–24 months creates pressure at the wrong time.
